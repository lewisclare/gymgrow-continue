What's working, and the 9 things holding BootyBox back

A lot already works. Every member has a written goal and a monthly review, you run a weekly check-in and a monthly event, members train 3 to 4 times a week, 3 in 4 women who come in for a consultation join, and you have 41 Google reviews at 5.0 stars. The problems sit in two places: too many members have left this year, and most women who enquire never get as far as coming in. Tap any finding to see the evidence.

01Too many members leave, so BootyBox has to sell far more just to growWhole plan
What this costs your goal

At about 7% leaving a month and about 6 joining, BootyBox stops growing at about 86 members however hard you sell. Your goals of 100, and then 140, can't be reached through sales alone.

Evidence
  • 17 members left and 19 joined in the last 3 months. That's 2 members of growth in 3 months.
  • 17 leaving in 3 months is about 7.3% of members a month. I aim for 4% at most, and 3% is excellent.
  • 10 members left in September, 7 in October and none so far in November. Only 2 of the 17 followed the coach who left to open her own facility; most of her members went in April and May. So most of today's leaving is something you can fix.
  • The reason members give most is "can't afford it".
  • At 7.3%, a member stays about 14 months on average and pays about £1,600 in that time. At 4% they stay about 25 months and pay about £2,900.
  • When a member asks to cancel, it happens over WhatsApp. You try to help, but there's no call and no set way to fix what's making them want to leave.
88members to replace a year at 7.3%, on 100 members
48at 4%. That's 40 fewer women to find, sell to and welcome each year

Replacing that many people each year costs more than money:

  • More ad spend and more of your time just to stand still, before any growth.
  • Coaches are always onboarding new faces instead of moving existing members on.
  • A less settled community. Small groups change faces, so fewer members build the friendships that keep them coming.
  • Fewer loyal members. Your 50 to 60 members who've been with you over a year are the ones who refer friends. A facility that keeps turning over has fewer of them.
My recommendation
  • Bring members leaving down step by step: 6.5% in December, 5.5% by February, 4% by May.
  • Every cancellation request gets a call that finds the real reason and offers a fix for it first, such as more accountability, adapted sessions or an easier way to fit training in. A cheaper option comes only if that doesn't work.
  • Keep the ads running while this happens, so BootyBox grows the whole way through.
