What's working, and the 7 things holding Empower back

A lot already works. You get about 70 leads a month from your ads at about £10 each, and you've gone from 1 or 2 new members a month to bringing beginners in every week. Three of the four funnel numbers are at or above our benchmarks: lead to booking 30%, show rate 62% and close rate 53%. In the last 7 days, new leads heard from you in about 3 hours on average, down from more than a day in October, which is exactly the fix you asked for. Members train 2.5 times a week, only 3 haven't been in for 14 days, and you have a core of members who've stayed for years. Every session is planned in writing, most of your jobs are written down, and a beginner gets an assessment, a plan within 48 hours and a week-3 review. The problems sit in four places: beginners who don't stay after their 28 days, a price that can't reach your revenue goal, a timetable bigger than your members need, and a gym that doesn't pay you yet. 01Fewer than half of your beginners become membersPhase 1
What this costs your goal

Getting intro to recurring from 44% to our 75% benchmark is about 2 to 3 more members a month, or about £235 more MRR added every month. Over 6 months that's most of the gap between 60 and 80 members.

Evidence
  • You counted 19 beginners in the last 3 months: 4 joined and 10 are still in their 28 days. Of the 9 who finished, 4 joined: 44%.
  • Grow Sales shows 4 of 5 (80%), because only 5 of those outcomes are marked. The other 4 beginners who finished have no outcome in Grow Sales, so the dashboard looks better than it is and we can't see who to follow up.
  • 21 people started your 28 days since 1 September, about 8 a month. This is the biggest number in your plan: every beginner who stays is a member you've already paid to find.
  • Your week-3 review is the right moment, and you already book it. You wrote that following up members' goals outside the gym has slipped recently, and that's what a beginner needs to see at week 3: proof it's working.

Source: questionnaire, questions 29, 34 and 54; Grow Sales, Empower, 1 Sep to 23 Nov 2026. Value: 31% more of about 8 beginners a month = about 2.5 members × £95 = about £235 MRR.

02Half of new leads waited more than a day to hear from youPhase 1
What this costs your goal

Your lead to booking is at our 30% benchmark, so the job is to hold it while you hand the calls over. If the wait creeps back up, bookings drop first.

Evidence
  • Since 1 September, 87 of the 184 leads who heard from a real person waited more than 24 hours. The middle lead waited about 22 hours. In October it was 28 hours.
  • In the last 7 days it's about 3 hours, and 11 of 12 leads heard from you within a day. That's a big change, and it came from you.
  • You do every call and consultation yourself, and you wrote that sales and client care are the first things you'd hand over.
  • 113 leads are sitting in Grow Sales with no booking yet.
  • Only 15 of 61 consultations held got a call the day before or that morning (25%). Your show rate is good anyway, at 62%, so this is a habit to keep it there rather than a leak.

Source: Grow Sales, Empower, 1 Sep to 23 Nov 2026, October 2026 and 17 to 23 Nov 2026; questionnaire, questions 15, 45, 50 and 51. My rule is a call from a real person within one working day.

03Your ads show a price and dates, and 3 of the 5 live ads show dates that have passedPhase 1
What this costs your goal

A price or a date in an ad makes people decide before they've spoken to you. Some rule themselves out on price, and a closing date that's passed tells them they've missed it.

Evidence
  • Two of your live ads say applications close 12 October, with a workshop on 17 October. A third says applications close 16 November, with a workshop on 21 November.
  • Most of your ads since September show the price of your 28 days in the text.
  • Your two newest ads ("Busy people" and "Women's 28 day kickstart") show no price and no dates. They're the model for the rest.
  • Your best ads are about a person and a feeling: mums putting themselves last (70 leads at about £8.50 each) and women who already train and want a new challenge (40 leads at about £8.40).

Source: Meta ads in Grow Sales, Empower, 28 Sep to 23 Nov 2026: £1,911 spent, 192 leads, about £10 a lead.

04At today's average fee, 80 members can't reach your revenue goalPhase 2
What this costs your goal

80 members at £77 is about £6,190 MRR. Your 6-month goal is £10,320 revenue. Beginners' 28 days and PT add some, but most of the gap has to come from what each member pays.

Evidence
  • Your MRR last month was £3,714 from 48 members: about £77 each. Adults average about £95, youth members about £25.
  • Members pay well under the list price: your £99 option averages £75, £129 averages £104 and £149 averages £104.
  • You've just moved to one price with 12-month, 6-month and rolling options, and you're moving current members onto it now.
  • The top reason members give for leaving is affordability. You wrote that past price changes caused "inconsistency and confusion". How you move members matters as much as the price.
  • Your 12-month goal (120 members, £15,500) needs about £129 a member, and so does your 6-month goal (80 members, £10,320).

Source: questionnaire, questions 9, 14, 16 to 21 and 25; call with Lewis, 17 Nov 2026. Sums: £3,714 ÷ 48 = £77; (£3,714 − 12 youth × £25.44) ÷ 36 adults = about £95; 80 × £77 = about £6,190; £10,320 ÷ 80 = £129.

05You run 35 classes a week, and about 2 people come to the average onePhase 3
What this costs your goal

Hours you coach to nearly empty rooms are hours you can't spend on beginners or sales. And a beginner who walks into a class of 2 doesn't feel the community your members stay for.

Evidence
  • 35 classes a week, with 8 to 16 spaces each. Average attendance across them is 2.4.
  • That's about 84 people a week in 280 to 560 spaces, so classes are 15% to 30% full.
  • Your Saturday morning freestyle calisthenics is the one that fills. It shows what works.
  • You pay cover coaches £20 to £25 a class, about £500 a month, and you coach 25 to 33 hours a week yourself.

Source: questionnaire, questions 37, 42, 57, 58 and 60. Sums: 35 × 2.4 = 84; 35 × 8 = 280 and 35 × 16 = 560 spaces.

07Your Instagram shows you, but not your members or what a session is likePhase 3
What this costs your goal

People who see your ads check your Instagram before they book. If they can't picture someone like them training at Empower, fewer book, and fewer of the ones who book turn up.

Evidence
  • 3,507 followers. 10 posts between 17 September and 21 November, about one a week.
  • Almost every recent post is you on your own: handstands, calisthenics skills, your sugar challenge, nutrition and training tips, your 4-year anniversary.
  • None shows a member training, a member's story in their own words, or what a beginner's first session looks like. Your ads talk to busy mums and women in menopause; your feed shows an advanced calisthenics coach.
  • What's good: your values come through clearly ("consistency over intensity", "fitness isn't one size fits all", "we are not a gym"), you're honest and human, and you don't sell in your posts. That's exactly right.
  • Instagram brought 2 leads in 3 months, against 197 from ads, and you rated it 2 out of 5 for getting strangers to join. Organic posts aren't for selling, but they should make a stranger curious to come in.

Source: Instagram @empower_movementandwellbeing, checked 23 Nov 2026; questionnaire, questions 47 and 66 to 70.
