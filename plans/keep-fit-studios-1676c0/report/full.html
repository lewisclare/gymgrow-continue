What this costs your goal

Many of the 190 women a month who see your ad look at your page before they book or turn up. A page that hasn't posted for a month gives them less reason to trust a studio they've never been to. I can't put a number on this one, so I'll measure it through show rate.

Evidence
  • I checked @keepfitstudios_glasgow on 23 November: 1,009 followers and 228 posts. The last post on your own page was on 20 October. Since then there's one reel shared with your coach Sophie, on 10 November.
  • Two of your last three posts end with a sales ask: "Dm us today" for the 28 days, and "click the link in our bio or send us a message". Your bio ends with "Join us today!". Instagram is for showing women who don't know you yet what Keep Fit stands for and what training there feels like. Your ads do the selling.
  • Some posts are notices for members, like "KFS UPDATE!!" and "Save the date: our next event", and one of your highlights is your timetable. Those belong in your members' group chat and the app. A woman who has never been in can't use them.
  • Your bio says "Helping people improve strength, fitness and lifestyle". It doesn't say you're female-only, the thing that makes Keep Fit different, even though your website, your ads and your highlights all do.
  • The good posts are already there: real members on the pads and lifting, a member's message about her shoulder feeling better, Kaiynat's results, and your women's event photo. Your highlights for female-only training, reviews and events are a strong start.
  • You rated your confidence that Instagram brings strangers in as 1 out of 5, and you told me you fell off your content calendar. Social media is one of the first jobs you'd hand over.
